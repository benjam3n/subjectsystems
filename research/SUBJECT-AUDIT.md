# Audit of the previous subject labels

The subsequent [scope definitions](../subjects/scopes/README.md) and [complete label mapping](SCOPE-MAP.md) now supply the concrete dispositions derived from this audit. Entity-kind differences alone do not disqualify a subject; its matter must be scoped.

Standing: Boundary analysis following the user's rejection of overlapping categories. All 39 legacy entries are covered; this table is an audit inventory, not a proposed set of peer classes.

The rows distinguish senses present in the existing pages, provide a concrete reason why their earlier placement did not establish exclusivity, and identify the resulting correction. They are design analyses at the stated scope, not an externally validated ontology. Ordinary labels can name more than one thing; a particular sense must be fixed before its membership conditions can be compared with another category.

An operation, object, role, property, and containing activity are also not declared five universally disjoint classes by this audit. They expose changes in the classification question that the earlier hierarchy concealed.

| Earlier label | What the label is doing | Overlap or ambiguity witness | Consequence for a precise hierarchy |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Action](../subjects/execution/action/README.md) | Occurrence, process, or execution of intended work. | Planning, writing, and searching may themselves be actions under the earlier broad scope. | Distinguish an occurrence from its description, intended result, and constituent operations. Action cannot remain their exclusive peer under that scope. |
| [Attention](../subjects/execution/attention/README.md) | Selection, allocation, focus, or persistence. | A selection of the next question can also be attention, relevance-sensitive choice, and an action. | Specify the allocation or control relation being studied and distinguish it from the selected object and the larger activity. |
| [Communication](../subjects/interaction/communication/README.md) | An interaction and the contributions made within it. | Asking a question, giving an explanation, prompting a model, and writing a reply can all be communication. | Separate the interaction, its constituent acts, their content, and their effects. Do not allocate the whole act by whichever contribution seems primary. |
| [Connections](../subjects/organization/connections/README.md) | Relations and consequences of those relations; formerly a proposed subject. | Preference, relevance, dependency, and representation already involve relations. | Define which relation instances and predicates are being classified before proposing exclusive relation kinds. Connections is not an established peer of its possible subtypes. |
| [Creativity](../subjects/creation/creativity/README.md) | Generation, development, a capacity to generate, or a quality of the result. | Creating a question or an explanation can also be reasoning, discovery, and writing. | Separate the object created, the creating operation, and the claimed capacity or novelty. Target-specific creation may sit below its subject. |
| [Curiosity](../subjects/inquiry/curiosity/README.md) | Interest, disposition, or activity directed toward finding out. | Wanting to answer a question may also be a goal; pursuing it may be inquiry or research. | Distinguish the interest or disposition from the question, commitment, and activity it supports. |
| [Decisions](../subjects/direction/decisions/README.md) | An act of commitment or the resulting commitment. | A judgment can be used to make a decision, and a written plan can express the commitment. | Choose whether the unit is the deciding act or its result; define commitment separately from a comparison, verdict, intention, or description. |
| [Design](../subjects/creation/design/README.md) | A proposed form, its construction, or the activity developing it. | An artifact can be a design and a plan, while designing it can also be problem solving. | Separate artifact, specification, and developing operation; derive boundaries for the intended sense rather than retaining the whole word as one type. |
| [Discovery](../subjects/inquiry/discovery/README.md) | A change in availability or understanding, the finding itself, or a process producing it. | A new question may be created and discovered; an old result may be discovered through retrieval. | Separate novelty of the object from novelty to an inquirer, the found object, and the operation making it available. |
| [Explanation](../subjects/understanding/explanation/README.md) | An account with an explanatory relation, or the act of constructing or communicating it. | One passage can be a claim, an explanation, a prediction, and a piece of writing. | Distinguish the account, what it explains, the explanatory relation, and its expression. Explanatory use alone does not exclude other uses of its content. |
| [Goals](../subjects/direction/goals/README.md) | An aim, desired condition, or commitment to pursue it. | A desired condition can also be stated as a claim and favored in a preference. | Separate the described condition from its being desired, adopted, pursued, or achieved. Establish which of those is the subject unit. |
| [Improvement](../subjects/development/improvement/README.md) | A change judged better under a stated comparison and standard; formerly proposed. | Learning can be improvement, but can also worsen performance; a new design can improve a result without learning. | Treat comparative benefit separately from the cause or method of change. Learning and Improvement are not exclusive alternatives. |
| [Intelligence](../subjects/development/intelligence/README.md) | Capacity, effective performance, or an account of how ability is used. | Learning, reasoning, and problem solving can manifest intelligence; available intelligence can also be treated as a resource. | Distinguish capacity, evidence of performance, the task performed, and a theory of their relation. A capability label cannot uniquely own its manifestations. |
| [Judgment](../subjects/direction/judgment/README.md) | An assessing operation, an applicable standard, or a verdict. | A truth assessment and a preference comparison can both yield judgments; a verdict may support a decision. | Separate standard, application, verdict, and subsequent commitment. Define the unit before comparing it with Decisions or Truth. |
| [Knowledge](../subjects/organization/knowledge/README.md) | Supported content, a state of knowing, or a body of retained work. | Known content can also be remembered, represented, explained, or used as a resource. | Distinguish content from its support, acceptance, availability, and being known by someone. Moving it to retention does not establish an exclusive type. |
| [Learning](../subjects/development/learning/README.md) | A change attributable to experience or earlier results. | The same change may be an improvement, a decline, or a change in memory or attention. | Specify what changes and how its relation to experience is established. Keep cause of change distinct from its quality or target. |
| [Memory](../subjects/organization/memory/README.md) | Retained material, retention, retrieval, reconstruction, or capacity. | A remembered claim can be knowledge, while a retrieval can also be search. | Separate stored content, the retaining relation, and the operations that recover or alter it. |
| [Philosophy](../subjects/inquiry/philosophy/README.md) | A broad domain of questions, accounts, and investigative practices. | Its work can concern truth, values, knowledge, reasoning, science, and nearly every earlier entry. | Derive particular subjects from definite questions and distinctions. The broad discipline name supplies no exclusive boundary against its contents. |
| [Planning](../subjects/execution/planning/README.md) | An operation constructing or revising possible later work. | Planning can be reasoning, design, and action; its result may be called a strategy. | Separate producing a plan from the plan and its execution. Establish plan-versus-strategy boundaries using properties rather than preferred labels. |
| [Prediction](../subjects/understanding/prediction/README.md) | An expectation, a claim concerning an outcome, or the operation producing it. | An explanatory model can also predict; a prediction can be a statement, assumption, or planning input. | Separate the content, its target and time conditions, its predictive use, and the generating operation. |
| [Preferences](../subjects/direction/preferences/README.md) | A favoring relation, contextual selection, or inferred pattern. | A preferred outcome can become a goal; a claim about a preference is also a claim. | Distinguish an actual comparison from its description, an inferred pattern, and a commitment based on it. |
| [Problem finding](../subjects/inquiry/problem-finding/README.md) | Recognition or formulation that makes a problem available. | Finding a problem can also create a question, discover a constraint, or begin solving it. | Distinguish the problem or problem formulation from the operations that make it available. Formulate target-specific changes instead of allocating the whole episode by relevance. |
| [Problem solving](../subjects/creation/problem-solving/README.md) | A composite activity addressing a difficulty. | It can include question formation, search, reasoning, planning, design, execution, and learning. | Separate the problem, proposed solution, interventions, and their outcomes; a composite workflow cannot be an exclusive peer of every activity it contains. |
| [Promoting](../subjects/unplaced/promoting/README.md) | Unresolved label with an earlier assistant interpretation about adoption. | No established definition supports a boundary against communication, influence, or prompting. | Withdraw from the candidate subject roster. Preserve the historical wording without substituting a marketing meaning or assuming a typo. |
| [Prompting](../subjects/interaction/prompting/README.md) | A prompt as an artifact, its use to govern model work, or construction of that prompt. | A prompt can contain a question or explanation, and sending it can be communication. | Separate content, instruction, use, and effect on model activity. Being used as a prompt does not make the content an exclusive intrinsic kind. |
| [Questions](../subjects/inquiry/questions/README.md) | A specification of what is asked, an interrogative expression, or an act of asking. | A question can be written, remembered, used as a resource, or created in a reasoning process. | Separate the question from its expression, occurrence, and operations. Question identity and answer conditions are candidates for the substantive boundary still to derive. |
| [Reasoning](../subjects/understanding/reasoning/README.md) | Transitions among claims, questions, assumptions, possibilities, and other material. | Generating a candidate explanation can also be creativity, discovery, and part of research. | Specify the individual transformation and its objects; do not use usefulness to assign the broad activity to one exclusive parent. |
| [Relevance](../subjects/execution/relevance/README.md) | A contribution relation in a context. | A question, claim, plan, or resource can all be relevant to the same inquiry. | Distinguish the contribution relation from the object that contributes and the inquiry receiving it. Relevance is not an intrinsic kind of its bearer. |
| [Representation](../subjects/organization/representation/README.md) | An expression, a represented object, a representing relation, or the operation constructing a form. | A plan, question, claim, or diagram may each be represented in text. | Separate content, form, and their relation. Medium and target cannot silently serve as competing sibling categories. |
| [Research](../subjects/inquiry/research/README.md) | An investigation composed of operations, findings, and continuations. | Research can include science, philosophy, writing, search, reasoning, and discovery. | Distinguish an investigation and its object from component operations and criteria governing the investigation. |
| [Resources](../subjects/execution/resources/README.md) | Something available for a use, or a relation between means and possible uses. | A question, memory, method, plan, or person can count as a resource in context. | A use-relative role cannot be an exclusive intrinsic type alongside the things that may fill it. Specify the resource relation or a genuinely bounded resource kind. |
| [Science](../subjects/inquiry/science/README.md) | An investigative practice, body of work, or account of how findings are established. | Scientific work can be research, explanation, reasoning, prediction, and discovery at once. | Separate the investigation, its contents, and the standards or methods attributed to it. No exclusive boundary follows from the broad field name. |
| [Search](../subjects/inquiry/search/README.md) | Operations for locating or generating candidates within a revisable space. | Retrieval can also be memory use; generating a candidate can also be creation. | Specify the target, candidate space, and actual state change. Distinguish locating an existing item from constructing an item or changing the space. |
| [Strategy](../subjects/execution/strategy/README.md) | An approach, policy, arrangement, or its development in context. | The same prospective arrangement may be called a plan, strategy, or design. | Identify a property that distinguishes the intended unit from plans and designs, or acknowledge that the labels partly denote the same objects. |
| [Truth](../subjects/understanding/truth/README.md) | A condition or property of what is asserted, or inquiry into that condition. | The same claim can be a prediction, explanation, and candidate for a truth judgment. | Distinguish the claim, its truth conditions, evidence, and acceptance. A property does not supply an exclusive peer category for its bearer. |
| [Uncertainty](../subjects/understanding/uncertainty/README.md) | Unresolved alternatives, a state relative to them, or a representation of that state. | An unanswered question, uncertain prediction, and undecided choice may each involve uncertainty. | Specify whose or what uncertainty, about which alternatives, and how it differs from the question or claim concerned. |
| [Understanding](../subjects/understanding/understanding/README.md) | An account, grasp of relations, capacity, or condition enabling use. | Explaining and predicting may express understanding; possession of an account may be described as knowledge. | Distinguish the account, possession of it, and demonstrated abilities. Do not equate the broad label with one exclusive type without those boundaries. |
| [Values](../subjects/direction/values/README.md) | Worth, importance, standards, or commitments concerning them. | A value can motivate a goal, be expressed as a preference, or act as a judgment criterion. | Separate a valued object, a favoring relation, and a reason or standard of worth. Resolve the intended sense before declaring it a peer of Goals or Preferences. |
| [Writing](../subjects/interaction/writing/README.md) | Construction of text and the intellectual contributions performed through it. | Writing can formulate questions, derive claims, construct explanations, or communicate decisions. | Separate the text, its semantic contents, its construction, and its use. Text medium does not exclude the operations or contents expressed through it. |

## What the audit changes

The earlier number of subjects does not determine the number of eventual subjects. A surviving word can require several precise senses; several words can partly name the same thing. A broad field can contain many narrower subjects, and an operation can require distinct versions for different targets. None of these cases is fixed by assigning one convenient main folder.

Questions versus question creation is developed in [DISTINCTIONS.md](../DISTINCTIONS.md). Its bounded record-change example shows what explicit exclusion conditions look like and why record identity is not yet a theory of question identity.

Promoting is withdrawn rather than carried forward as an obligatory category awaiting a conventional definition. Its historical entry remains linked above for provenance. The remaining 38 entries preserve substantive material; this audit does not admit them as 38 mutually exclusive classes.

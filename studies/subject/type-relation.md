| Relation | Condition |
|---|---|
| Subject type inclusion | Every member of A is a member of B under the same interpretation. |
| Subject type inclusion refutation | At least one member of A is not a member of B. |
| Subject type exclusion | No member of A is a member of B. |
| Subject type overlap | At least one member belongs to both A and B. |
| Subject type equivalence | Every A is B and every B is A under the same interpretation. |
| Subject qualification inheritance | A qualified subclass inherits its containing class's established inclusions and exclusions. |
| Subject relation reversal | The proposed B-to-A inclusion is examined separately from A-to-B inclusion. |

| Comparison | Case | Relation |
|---|---|---|
| Development; Improvement | A structure can be elaborated while performance worsens; a defect can be removed without elaboration. | Overlap under the stated readings. |
| Intelligence; Improvement | Retained adaptive ability differs from a change producing comparative benefit. | Different classified items. |
| Intelligence; Creation | Choosing an existing adequate solution differs from constructing a new one. | Capability, exercise, and product have different identities. |
| Planning; Strategy | Both names can denote the same prospective course structure. | Equivalent under that shared reading. |
| Production; type membership | A method can produce an improvement without itself being an improvement. | Causal contribution differs from subtype inclusion. |

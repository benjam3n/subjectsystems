# Subjects as scopes of consideration

A subject is **what is being considered**: a matter of attention, inquiry, or work. An object, process, property, relation, possibility, or question can be a subject. Subject is a role in consideration, not one intrinsic kind of entity.

The previous analysis was useful about the need for boundaries but too quick to treat mixed entity types as the root problem. Calling creation an operation does not disqualify creation as a subject. The question is which matter “Creation” includes and whether another alternative subject includes exactly that same matter.

The latest direction also makes the names literal: general subject first, then the target and determination, without joining distinct subjects by “and.” The [named inventory](subjects/scopes/NAMES.md) is current; the [name-change map](subjects/scopes/NAME-CHANGES.md) retains the earlier labels. A lexical prefix is not a proof of entity subtype or non-overlap.

The user asked to make that scope explicit. The [working definitions](subjects/scopes/README.md) now state what each included scope settles, what it excludes, a boundary case, and a substantive next result. The [label mapping](research/SCOPE-MAP.md) records the consequences for every earlier word.

## What the hierarchy classifies

The unit is a **specified matter to be settled**, with the target and consequential context fixed. A document, person, method, or episode can contain many such matters. The hierarchy does not claim to partition those whole containers.

The useful distinction is between what the answer must determine and the things mentioned or used in obtaining it. A question can be used to investigate a preference. That does not make the preference matter part of question specification. A claim can express an aim; that does not make every goal inquiry a study of claim semantics.

This is a way to define scopes, not a mandatory question form or a universal data model for every response. Context can already make the matter clear. Do not require users to classify their requests before work can proceed.

## What non-overlap requires

- A child scope is contained in its parent: every matter admitted by the child is admitted by the parent.
- Alternative branches must not admit the same fully specified matter in the same respect and context.
- An exhaustive claim requires every in-scope matter to be covered. The present atlas makes no claim to cover every possible subject.
- Ambiguous wording is not automatically a collision. Resolve the competing interpretations and compare the actual matters.
- A single well-specified matter satisfying two sibling definitions is a collision. Narrow, merge, or otherwise repair those definitions.

Parents in the working model denote the union of their defined children. Their short names do not add all ordinary associations of those words to their scope. Stipulating a parent union establishes containment; it does not by itself prove that the child definitions exclude each other. That requires the boundary reasoning.

Separate constituent matters only where there really are separately determinable results. A single compatibility or coupling question can require its own relation scope. Do not evade a collision by giving the same result two names or declaring a primary home.

## Questions and creation

If Questions means everything concerning questions, and Creation means everything concerning creation, question creation belongs to both. One folder cannot fix this.

The working Questions scope is specifically question specification and answer fit. Creation of questions is a narrower investigation within question specification: it develops the requested distinction, referents, assumptions, and resolution conditions. General intellectual construction is separately scoped to principles across target kinds. It does not claim all local creation tasks.

This is containment of matters considered. It does not assert that a creation event is a kind of question. Likewise, developing a system within a subject does not make the system a subtype of the subject's object. Scope containment, system composition, source lineage, and result consumption remain distinct relations.

Question identity needs substantive work. Different wording can preserve a request; identical wording can change its referent; recovery can make an old question newly available. A record identifier cannot settle these distinctions.

The earlier record-change example remains valid at its limited scope: absent→present, present→changed-present, and present→absent distinguish introduction, revision, and removal of one identified record. It does not decide whether revising a question record preserves the question itself. That must be established from the requested distinction and its conditions.

## Definitions with actual consequences

| Boundary | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Question specification / answer fit | A question can be well specified while a response fails to address it. |
| Answer fit / truth or support | A response can supply the requested kind of answer while being false or unsupported. |
| Goal / commitment / course | A candidate aim can be specified before adoption; an adopted aim can have several possible plans. |
| Preference / standard / assessment | Observed favoring, justified criteria, and applying those criteria are different results. |
| Planning / Strategy | Their shared prospective arrangement is merged; no exclusive distinction is invented from the names. |
| Learning / Improvement | Experience-dependent change and comparative merit are different relations, with improvement nested within assessment. |
| Recovery / discovery increment | Continuity with earlier material and novelty to the present inquirer can vary independently. |
| Remedy adequacy / actual effect | A specified effect can be sufficient for a problem while the proposed intervention still fails to produce it. |

The [reviewed cases](cases/subject-boundaries.md) explain these distinctions and an actual collision repaired during this derivation. The result is a first usable model, with exposed limits, rather than another list of undefined words or a claim of a perfected ontology.

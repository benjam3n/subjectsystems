# Distinguishable subjects

Standing: Non-overlapping subject categories are the user's current requirement. The distinctions and worked examples below develop that requirement; they do not establish a complete replacement taxonomy.

## What failed

The eight functional groups classified subjects by what they could contribute. Reasoning can contribute to discovery, design, choice, communication, and improvement. Its contribution therefore cannot determine an exclusive parent. Giving it a main folder and adding connections elsewhere made storage unique while leaving category membership overlapping.

The original list also combined several different questions: what something is, what is done to it, what property it has, what role it plays, and which larger activity contains it. Regrouping that list preserved the error. The list is now an inventory to examine; its entries are not guaranteed places in the final hierarchy.

## What non-overlap requires

For each proposed division, the classified thing must be specified. A question definition, an act of asking it, the sentence that expresses it, and a workflow that produces it are distinguishable candidates for classification. A whole conversation is not the same unit as one of its constituent objects or changes.

Within a containment hierarchy:

- Every child satisfies its parent's definition.
- Siblings divide the same kind of thing using explicit membership conditions.
- The same classified item cannot satisfy two sibling definitions in the same respect and context.
- A claimed exhaustive division accounts for every item within its stated scope. Unresolved cases remain unresolved; they are not assigned by closest fit.

If a parent denotes a set P and its children denote C1, C2, and so on, each child is contained in P and the intersection of any two siblings is empty. The union of the children equals P only when the division is claimed to be exhaustive. Parent and child share scope by containment; exclusivity applies between alternative branches, not between a class and its subclasses.

One sibling set cannot silently mix target, operation, medium, quality, and purpose. A written explanation can be writing, communication, reasoning, and an explanation when those labels ask different questions about it. That is a counterexample to treating those labels as exclusive sibling categories. It is not repaired by choosing whichever label seems most important.

## Questions and creation

Questions names a possible subject matter. Creation names a kind of change whose target remains unspecified. Their relationship becomes definite in **creation of questions**.

| Expression | Relation actually asserted |
|---|---|
| Question creation | A specified operation creates a question |
| Goal creation | A specified operation creates a goal |
| Creating a question about a goal | The operation creates a question; its content concerns a goal |
| Using an existing question to develop a goal | A question is used in an operation that develops a goal |
| A workflow that creates a question and a goal | The workflow contains distinct contributions; classifying the whole by only one output would conceal the other |

This supports studying question creation within Questions. It does not place all creation below Questions. A strict type hierarchy can instead have Question operations as a parent and precisely defined kinds of those operations below it. A subject page can contain that operation hierarchy without implying that an operation is itself a kind of question. Containment of documentation, part of a system, acts on an object, and is a subtype must remain explicit relations.

Nor is the entire proposed replacement now `objects / operations / properties / methods`. Those broad labels would need their own boundaries. An operation is also an event under some descriptions, and a method can itself be studied as an object. This diagnosis is not a finished partition of everything.

## What question identity must preserve

Consider a question about the start time of a particular meeting. The following are constructed boundary cases, not results of a deployed system.

| Change | What the case shows |
|---|---|
| One person learns the meeting's start time while another still does not know it | The question can remain the same while the person's uncertainty changes. A question is not identical to that person's uncertainty. |
| “When does this meeting start?” becomes “What is the start time of this meeting?”, with the same referent and intended precision | Different expressions can specify the same question. Wording alone does not individuate it. |
| The words stay the same but “this meeting” refers to a different event | Identical words can specify different questions. Reference and context can affect identity. |
| The same question is recovered from an earlier notebook | Its availability to the reader changes; a newly available question is not necessarily newly created. |
| “When does the meeting start?” is replaced by “Will the meeting occur?” | The required distinction changes. A time answer and an occurrence answer satisfy different requests. |

These cases rule out several candidate definitions. The classified question cannot simply be its sentence, an act of asking, an inquirer's uncertainty, or the event of discovering it. A candidate account concerns the distinction to be resolved, its referents, and what would count as resolving it. That account still needs work for open answer spaces, presuppositions, requested examples, and changes of scope; these cases do not prove its sufficiency.

Three operations already become distinguishable in these cases: forming a question that was not there, producing another expression of an existing question, and making an existing question available to someone. The same workflow may contain all three, but that does not make the three changes identical. A system claiming question creation must establish more than new wording or new availability.

This is the kind of substantive distinction the hierarchy must encode. It is stronger than choosing a folder for Questions, and it also changes how a custom question-creation system would have to work.

## A bounded example of an exclusive division

Consider elementary changes to one explicitly identified question record. The record contains its question definition, including its stated scope and answer conditions. An elementary change has a before state and an after state and affects that record's existence or definition. It is not a whole inquiry or an arbitrary conversation turn.

| Kind of change | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Record absent | Record present |
| Revision | Record present | Same record present with a changed definition |
| Removal | Record present | Record absent |

Within that declared scope these conditions exclude each other. A no-change read is outside the parent because it does not change the record. A sequence that introduces one record and revises another is a composition of two changes. Retrieving an existing question does not by itself create that question; creating a local record of it and recovering its content are separate events.

This example establishes a partition of specified record changes. It does not settle the deeper identity of a question. Rephrasing may preserve the question; changing its presupposition or answer conditions may create a different question. A database identifier cannot decide that conceptual issue. The distinction between revising an existing question and replacing it with another still needs an account of question identity when the classified unit is the question itself.

The example shows the required standard: a declared unit, a fixed distinction, mutually exclusive conditions, and exact limits. It is not evidence that the original 39 subjects are disjoint.

## Consequences for the existing names

| Earlier entries | Distinction that the old hierarchy hid |
|---|---|
| Questions and Creativity | The target created versus an operation or capacity that can create many kinds of target |
| Knowledge and Memory | Supported content or a state of knowing versus retained material, retention, or retrieval; the intended senses need separation |
| Explanation and Writing | An account's explanatory role versus the medium or activity that constructs and expresses it |
| Planning and Action | Constructing a plan versus performing an occurrence; planning may itself be an action under the broad earlier scope |
| Learning and Improvement | A change attributable to experience versus a change judged better; learning can improve or worsen performance |
| Relevance and Resources | Contribution to a purpose and availability for a use are contextual relations or roles; they do not define exclusive intrinsic kinds of their bearers |
| Goals and Preferences | A pursued aim versus a favoring relation; a preference can support a goal without being that goal |
| Truth and Discovery | A claim's truth and a change in what becomes available are different matters; a discovery process does not establish truth by its name |

The [complete label audit](research/SUBJECT-AUDIT.md) examines every legacy entry. Some names may survive with precise scope; others may split, move below another subject, describe operations or properties, or leave the hierarchy. The old roster and the number eight impose no requirement on that derivation.

## Promotion

The earlier assistant proposal included Promoting with branches about attention, encounter, adoption, and continued use. That interpretation had no established subject definition from the user. The word also appears in the user's earlier relevance example, so neither a typo nor an exclusively assistant-originated word is established by the available evidence.

The latest user says they do not understand promotion. Promoting is therefore withdrawn from the candidate subject list; its record remains only to explain the earlier mistake. No marketing or influence subject is inserted in its place. See the [historical proposal](sources/reviews/subject-sciences-proposal-2026-09-06.md) and [withdrawn entry](subjects/unplaced/promoting/README.md).

## Current result

The shared-work classification is rejected. The non-overlap requirement is established. The audit identifies concrete failures and distinguishes the Questions/creation relation. A complete exclusive subject hierarchy has not yet been derived. The old folders remain addresses for retained material, with their rejected classification status explicit; they are not the current intellectual architecture.

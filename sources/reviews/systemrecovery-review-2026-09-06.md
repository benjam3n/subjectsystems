**System Recovery: review, enduring goals, and the next research program**

6 September 2026. Review of `benjam3n/systemrecovery` at `d71a8430452fb8563d722c5df3b904bb7109197e`, checked again against `main` before preparing this report. Comparison with `benjam3n/reasoningtool2` at `ae2435b0004ac44de9bb09c2f045805722038dbb`, selected GOSM goal-journey reports, saved September 6 plans, and retrieved recent conversation context. This is a review and proposed handoff; it does not report repository changes or adoption of the recommendations.

**The decision**

Continue from the existing recovery. Its preservation, native profiles, typed relationships, distinctions about evidence, and source-reading tools already provide substantial infrastructure. The next program should make that material participate in the selection, construction, continuation, and revision of actual intellectual work. Recent conversations also expand the subject: what determines an intellectual contribution, how those determinants can be changed, and what new forms of inquiry and action become possible afterward.

The most consequential gap is the connection between accumulated understanding and the work chosen next. This review found several different causes within that connection: a current operating contract is absent from the portable source pack; newer proposals conflict with that contract; anticipation is represented mainly as preference prediction; and the source graph does not implement semantic or task-dependent reconsideration. These findings support specific changes. They do not establish that a new general orchestrator is required.

The stronger interpretation of the request is: reconstruct the intended system from the best available work; identify what exists, what has been superseded, and what has never been made available; examine the system using the newer operations; establish purposes that outlive its present architecture; and prepare work whose results determine what happens next. That interpretation remains connected to the request rather than substituting an invented personal destination.

**The findings that change the next work**

**1. Some missing understanding is already present elsewhere in the current system.**

Reasoningtool2's `CURRENT.md` already distinguishes missing material from failures of finding, access, relevance recognition, representation, connection, selection, use, retention, and reuse. It permits criterion revision, new methods, exploration before diagnosis, and changes to the question. `METHODS.md` explicitly describes conditional examples rather than a complete routing table.

The recovery pack contains detailed predecessor material but omits these current documents. That produces a specific risk: an agent can faithfully read the recovery guide while missing the working contract that should constrain its synthesis. The first correction is to make that contract reachable from the recovery's current entry point and identify its authority. Recreating those ideas as a new taxonomy would add duplication.

The review itself changed direction on this evidence. The initial candidate diagnosis was missing operation-selection guidance. Reading the current contract narrowed it to incomplete exposure, conflicting proposals, and untested use of guidance that already exists.

Sources: [current contract](https://github.com/benjam3n/reasoningtool2/blob/ae2435b0004ac44de9bb09c2f045805722038dbb/CURRENT.md), [conditional methods](https://github.com/benjam3n/reasoningtool2/blob/ae2435b0004ac44de9bb09c2f045805722038dbb/METHODS.md).

**2. Integration needs to reconcile authority as well as meaning.**

The September 6 corpus-sweep hierarchy declares a closed set of 23 verbs, treats YAML procedures as canonical and skills as renderings, prescribes a common project contract, and selects a method partly by the cheapest missing input. The current contract explicitly rejects a fixed operation inventory, default execution of the former closed dispatcher, mandatory per-turn forms, and cheapness as sufficient priority.

This is a conflict between a current contract and a proposed organization. The sweep's later date does not itself revoke the contract. Nor does preserving a source imply adopting it. The structural supplement already says historical instructions do not automatically authorize actions; the missing application is to expose these particular conflicts where an agent begins work.

A current integration record should retain each proposal, its source, whether the user adopted it, where it applies, and which governing instruction controls the present task. This is also a direct application of the recent role-change idea: the same text behaves differently as an example, a historical proposal, a premise, or an instruction.

The sweep's rule that zero recorded world contacts makes an artifact a draft also needs reconsideration before adoption. Completion must be relative to the requested object. A proof, conceptual distinction, or completed design has different completion conditions from deployment or an observed external intervention. Conversely, recording external contact does not establish that an artifact answered its question.

**3. The anticipation register covers a different target from the recent anticipation work.**

`ANTICIPATION.md` predicts Ben's likely preferences, objections, and collaboration needs. That is useful evidence about a recipient. The recent work also concerns the assistant's own prospective contribution: what it expects to do, which starting conditions select that contribution, whether it can choose another operation, and which continuations its response makes possible or unnecessary.

Those targets should remain distinct. Predicting that Ben dislikes filler does not supply an operation that prevents filler. Predicting a good response and then producing it does not establish prediction accuracy independently, because the anticipation can influence production.

The addition should include concrete cases of prospective contribution selection and execution, with the current objective, candidate contributions, expected consequences, actual contribution, and consequential difference. A prediction need not be displayed to the user on every turn. The value is in changing what gets produced and preserving enough evidence to assess that change.

**4. An unmet-condition workflow is one important route through a larger inquiry.**

`ARTIFACT-METHOD.md` explicitly says that the unmet condition selects the work. It already allows design examples to precede requirements and recognizes conceptual understanding as a legitimate result. It should retain those strengths.

The newer work adds trials whose contribution is discovered through trying them. A candidate can reveal an unrecognized criterion. A perspective can expose a question that the previous description could not express. A successful result can eliminate the work that was supposed to follow it. Requiring every such move to be justified by a previously specified deficiency misrepresents how that move became available.

For example, a report could satisfy all its specified facts and format requirements. Recasting the same material around a disputed inference can expose an unresolved assumption that none of those requirements requested. The new assumption test arises from the recasting. The prior gates were satisfied within their scope; their scope did not exhaust worthwhile inquiry.

This does not establish that departures always help. It establishes a place for purpose-directed exploration whose result can revise the criteria and plan. Grounds for trying an operation and evidence that it helped are separate judgments.

Source: [artifact method](https://github.com/benjam3n/systemrecovery/blob/d71a8430452fb8563d722c5df3b904bb7109197e/ARTIFACT-METHOD.md).

**5. Preserving native structure and changing the working frame are compatible obligations.**

The structural supplement correctly preserves distinctions among containment, prerequisites, routes, derivations, revisions, and evidence. The theory-of-perspectives work asks an additional question: which alternative organizations expose consequences that a native view hides?

A source-faithful representation can coexist with several competing working representations. A translation should state what it preserves and loses. A newly useful perspective should be allowed to revise the categories used for current work without rewriting the historical source into that interpretation.

There are therefore two different fidelities: fidelity to what the source actually contains, and fidelity to the subject or goal being investigated. They can come apart. An old method can be imitated faithfully and still be the wrong method for the present inquiry. A justified adaptation can depart from the source and improve the task while remaining an adaptation, rather than being falsely presented as the original.

Source: [reorganization](https://github.com/benjam3n/systemrecovery/blob/d71a8430452fb8563d722c5df3b904bb7109197e/structure/REORGANIZATION.md).

**6. The graph preserves relations; cumulative inquiry requires using changes in those relations.**

The schema explicitly states that automatic semantic invalidation and cross-version identity reconciliation are unimplemented. Hashes detect changed bytes. They do not establish whether an unchanged source is still relevant, whether a changed criterion alters its use, or whether a new interpretation withdraws an inference.

A later finding can affect several different things: the truth of a conclusion, its evidential support, its relevance to a task, the priority of that task, or the admissibility of an operation. These effects require different repairs. A conclusion with independent support can survive the loss of one argument. A true conclusion can become irrelevant without becoming false. A task can become unnecessary even when its premises remain true.

Existing dependency tooling should be reused for explicitly recorded dependencies. Extend representation only for a consequential case it cannot express. A universal graph schema is not a prerequisite for carrying through one real correction.

Sources: [schema limits](https://github.com/benjam3n/systemrecovery/blob/d71a8430452fb8563d722c5df3b904bb7109197e/structure/SCHEMA.md), [current correction contract](https://github.com/benjam3n/reasoningtool2/blob/ae2435b0004ac44de9bb09c2f045805722038dbb/CURRENT.md).

**Goals that should survive changes in the project**

These are enduring directions and standards for the undertaking described in the request. They are not claims that every capability is already achieved, that the same actions are appropriate in every state, or that Ben's eventual priorities cannot change.

**1. Make accumulated understanding available at the point where it can change work.** A useful discovery should retain its meaning, conditions, source, and route into later inquiry. Success includes recovering an overlooked alternative or a previously rejected inference before it has to be rediscovered through another correction.

**2. Discover and pursue worthwhile questions, including questions the present framework cannot yet formulate.** The project must be able to reconsider its problem descriptions, omitted alternatives, relevant distinctions, and criteria of importance. Its inquiry space cannot be limited to whichever categories were easiest to encode first.

**3. Construct and revise the means of thinking.** Operations, operation selection, composition rules, representations, and evaluation practices are all legitimate objects of discovery. The desired capability includes generating a needed method and replacing an inadequate one. A finite catalog can support that work without claiming to exhaust it.

**4. Carry substantive aims through connected work.** The system should determine and perform consequential next steps, integrate their results, revise its plan, and resume after interruption. It should reduce repeated human repair of transitions while remaining responsive to human direction. A finished investigation can stop; continuity is not endless continuation.

**5. Produce advances in the subjects that matter.** Systematic philosophy, explanations of thinking, writing, research, design, and practical projects each have their own substantive goals. Reorganizing their vocabulary is progress only where that reorganization yields understanding or capability relevant to them. Inquiry and experience can also be valued directly.

**6. Make successful work improve subsequent work.** A correction should affect the next applicable occasion. A new operation should become available when its conditions recur. An improved means of inquiry should help discover another improvement or advance a substantive subject. The record of a lesson and evidence that the lesson was used remain distinct.

**7. Use new capability to enlarge the range of possible futures.** After a capability is gained, examine what becomes feasible, what remains worth wanting, and which new dependencies matter. This includes human learning and agency, creative and intellectual practice, collaboration, and eventually broader arrangements for learning and collective action where desired. Present infrastructure does not define the ceiling of the program.

**8. Preserve the ability to replace the system itself.** Useful understanding, evidence, decisions, and goals should survive changes in repositories, models, interfaces, and methods. The project succeeds if a better arrangement absorbs its useful functions. Maintaining the current architecture is conditional on its contribution.

**Standards for every state of development**

| Standard | What must remain true of the work |
|---|---|
| Current authority remains explicit | Historical proposals and retrieved instructions do not silently override the user's current request or the current working contract |
| Claims retain their identity and scope | A counterexample addresses the proposition actually asserted; a conditional result does not silently become universal |
| Unknown is not absent | A search miss, an omitted graph edge, and an unimplemented capability remain distinguishable |
| Evidence keeps its kind | Occurrence, derivation, execution, acceptance, and benefit are not interchangeable success labels |
| Criteria remain examinable | A passed test does not forbid questioning whether that test addresses the purpose |
| Revision preserves justified work | A changed premise reopens its affected uses without erasing independent support or unrelated results |
| Exploration remains available | A useful trial need not begin with proof that the current approach is wrong |
| Work stays connected to chosen purposes | More files, graph edges, tests, or autonomous steps do not substitute for the relevant intellectual or practical contribution |
| The user retains direction | Inferred preferences and future scenarios support choices without becoming hidden commitments |
| Organization earns its cost | Added records and interfaces should resolve a recurring retrieval, distinction, continuation, or revision need |

A global goal and a local operation have different jobs. “Make accumulated understanding usable” can remain a goal for years. The next operation might be retrieving one document, withdrawing one inference, constructing a new distinction, or deleting redundant scaffolding. A durable goal does not imply a permanent work queue.

**How to re-examine the collection from this understanding**

The next review should follow important uses through the collection. For each selected inquiry, reconstruct the source material, the role it played, the operation performed, the result, and what changed afterward. This exposes losses that file inventories cannot: lost ordering, omitted conditions, detached examples, a rejected explanation promoted back into authority, or an intermediate result that no later task consumes.

Use several views of the same case:

| View | What the review examines | A consequential finding |
|---|---|---|
| Recovery | Which needed object exists, where it is, and how it is reached | The current contract exists but the portable pack omits it |
| Operations | What transformation was actually performed and what it preserved | An archived proposal became a governing constraint during synthesis |
| Anticipation | Which contribution was expected, selected, and actually produced | The agent expected to add missing methods, then source retrieval justified reconciling authority instead |
| Perspectives | Which descriptions make different questions or moves available | A provenance graph answers origin questions while a working inquiry view exposes obsolete next tasks |
| Goal journey | What the result enables and which future work it changes | Successful recovery shifts attention to use, method construction, and substantive inquiry |
| Allocation | Which competing work receives effort and why | Bulk migration is deferred because a specific contradiction changes the next deliverable more directly |

These views can overlap. Their usefulness is established by the distinctions and consequences they expose in the case. Merely restating a case six times does not satisfy the comparison.

Missingness should then be reported at the relevant location: source not found; source outside the inspected scope; source found but inaccessible to the current agent; source misunderstood; instruction status unclear; operation not specified; operation specified but not selected; selected but incorrectly executed; result obtained but not retained; retained but not used; or the wrong question being pursued. Several can coexist. This is a starting set of distinctions, not a final ontology.

**The selected inquiry and its first prepared cases**

Central question: **What determines which intellectual contribution a capable system makes, and how can those determinants themselves become deliberately changeable?**

The immediate worked case can begin from the recent ontology revision: dependence on another thing was used as a reason to exclude a relation from the basic ontology. Withdrawing that reason reopens the candidate without proving it belongs in the ontology. The earlier derivation is the starting material; the next work concerns the change to the inquiry and its continuation.

| Case | Decisive condition | Work warranted by that condition | Near miss |
|---|---|---|---|
| Withdraw an exclusion reason | The reason no longer supports exclusion | Reopen candidates excluded solely by that reason; preserve independent reasons for exclusion | Declaring the reopened candidate true |
| Discover a new criterion through a trial | The trial distinguishes outcomes the prior criterion treated alike | Determine whether the distinction matters to the purpose and revise the criterion where justified | Calling the prior artifact false simply because the criterion changed |
| Anticipate a contribution | Several possible operations could follow from the current question | Compare their effects on the live uncertainty; perform one and inspect its actual consequence | Predicting a sophisticated answer and producing generic explanation |
| Exclude an anticipated continuation | A familiar continuation is suspended while the substantive purpose remains | Construct another contribution that still bears on that purpose | Producing irrelevant novelty to satisfy the exclusion |
| Compose operations | One operation creates, removes, or changes an input used by another | Compare the orders where the dependency differs | Treating two method names in sequence as evidence of successful composition |
| Resume after a correction | Prior material is available, but a changed premise affects some of its uses | Resume from the revised state and propagate the relevant change | Summarizing the correction while continuing the old plan |
| Act after success | The current objective has been achieved sufficiently for its purpose | Retain the result and determine which newly feasible inquiry or action warrants pursuit | Repeating the solved developmental stage because the roadmap lists it |

These are prepared distinctions, not empirical model-performance results. The first sustained run should include an ambiguous case where choosing among them requires actual judgment. A list of easy examples cannot establish reliable selection.

A useful retained transition would identify: the inquiry before the move; what was uncertain; the operation; its actual result; the next work changed by that result; and what remains open. Record only consequential transitions. The agent should perform and organize the work rather than present this as a form for Ben to fill out.

**What an interface would need to make possible**

A new application is not established as necessary by this review. The existing file and browser tools can support the first cases. An interface becomes justified when recurring work needs interactions that they do not support adequately.

The useful interface is organized around a live inquiry. It should make it possible to select a claim and see its source, current role, support, challenges, affected work, and earlier revisions; hold several representations of the same material side by side; change a premise and inspect the consequences; retrieve a complete method example when needed; and resume from the actual frontier.

The model supplies judgments about relevance, implications, alternatives, and what to do next. Code can preserve identities and versions, retrieve evidence, execute available checks, and propagate explicitly recorded dependencies. This division remains provisional: a task may justify more code or less. Encoding a choice does not establish that the encoded criterion is the right one.

Interface tests should be tasks such as: recover the reason an approach was rejected; revise one assumption without losing independent results; identify the first substantive divergence between two perspectives; or continue after an interruption without repeating the same correction. Counts of panels, visual graph density, and the number of available buttons do not answer those questions.

**The longer journey, including success**

The following horizons are planning containers from the original Future Analysis procedure. They are neither deadlines nor forecasts of model capabilities. Later capabilities should be used whenever they are already available.

| Horizon | Work the program should be prepared to support | What success changes |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 years | Sustained intellectual work whose useful results and methods remain available across inquiries | Repeated repair consumes less effort; harder questions and broader applications become feasible |
| 3–7 years | Coordinated research and construction across connected subjects; generation and revision of methods needed by those projects | Selecting among valuable projects and reconciling their consequences becomes more important |
| 8–20 years | Transfer of established understanding through new models, tools, representations, and, where wanted, collaborative settings | Particular repositories and prompting conventions can be replaced while purposes and valid findings survive |
| 20+ years | Continued access to accumulated understanding and freedom to choose new intellectual, practical, and collective ends | The present organization becomes one historical means within a changing pursuit |

Under continuation, useful local work remains possible while the user supplies many transitions. The response is to preserve and address the specific transitions that repeatedly require repair. Under acceleration, results increasingly determine subsequent work; the response is to apply the gained capability to more consequential questions and compare competing opportunities. Under disruption, a new model, an invalidated premise, or a better representation makes some machinery obsolete; the response is to reconsider the affected functions while retaining independently useful results.

All three constructed scenarios support recoverability, revisable means, explicit purposes, and substantive use. Agreement among these scenarios is not a probability estimate and does not establish that they exhaust the future.

**Stress tests of the review's recommendations**

These are review hypotheses, not statements attributed to Ben. The factual source findings above were checked directly. The following analytic claims were examined after source inspection; imported source limits are identified as such and are not presented as independent discoveries.

**C1. Giving an agent access to the latest written corrections is sufficient to prevent recurrence of those errors.**

- F1, AR from C1: when the relevant correction is available, the corresponding error cannot recur under the claim's stated sufficiency.
- F2, AR from F1: selecting or executing the wrong rule despite access is excluded by C1, even without evidence that retrieval changed selection.
- F3, AW to C1: a constructed agent reads the correction, retains an incompatible historical rule as governing, and repeats the error. Access is present; the claimed prevention is absent.
- F4, alternative from F3: access and use of the correction are distinct causal conditions.
- F5, AR from F4: adding another accessible copy does not change that constructed case unless it changes which rule is used.
- F6, countercase to making extra control always necessary: an agent already applies corrections reliably once the missing source is supplied; additional machinery contributes no necessary repair in that case.
- F7, result from F4–F6: remedy the observed transition. Access may be the sufficient local repair; access alone is not a general guarantee.

Certificate: C1 is rejected as a universal sufficiency claim by F3. The strongest contrary branch, an agent already using supplied corrections reliably, survives as a narrower case under F6. The actual contribution of additional control remains unmeasured.

**C2. Every useful next operation answers an unmet condition already specified before the operation begins.**

- F8, AR from C2: a newly useful operation must be assignable to a previously specified unmet condition.
- F9, AR from F8: discovery of a new criterion cannot be the first basis for judging a trial useful; an earlier specified deficiency must already justify its role.
- F10, AW to C2: a constructed artifact passes the stated criteria; a purpose-related recasting exposes a consequential assumption and a new criterion. The trial is useful while no earlier specified unmet condition names its contribution.
- F11, alternative from F10: some operations generate the criteria or distinctions that make their consequences evaluable.
- F12, AR from F11: the reason to try such an operation can precede the evidence by which its actual contribution is judged.
- F13, AW to an unrestricted extension of F11: a trial creates an arbitrary criterion favoring itself while leaving the substantive purpose unchanged and unserved. Criterion creation alone does not make that trial useful.
- F14, result from F12–F13: exploratory justification and retrospective evaluation need distinct support; neither a prior failure nor a self-created success label universally settles them.

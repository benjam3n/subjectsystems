## Local purpose

Develop a concrete artifact whose form and requirements can be discovered through encountering alternatives.

## What changes from the source

Make candidates concrete enough for the relevant experience, track selected aspects, and distinguish preference feedback from feasibility evidence. Produce a design plus justified requirements and unresolved construction conditions.

## Objects and distinctions

Artifact candidates or prototypes; intended use; selected aspects; design variations; candidate requirements; feasibility constraints; validation evidence.

## Operations

1. Produce materially different forms of the artifact at the fidelity needed for the choice. Include an alternative that changes the assumed form where that could alter the result.
2. Present or exercise the candidates in the relevant context. Record whole-candidate selections separately from comments about a particular aspect.
3. Generate variations that preserve the favored aspect while changing another consequential property. Include a contrast that could show the favored aspect was incidental.
4. Derive candidate requirements from the selections and stated use. Preserve their evidence and the distinction between a necessary condition and one attractive implementation.
5. Assess the factual constraints of the candidate through the appropriate inspection or test. Approval and buildability receive separate results.
6. Revise the design when feasibility changes; revise the requirement when the changed design exposes a mistaken interpretation. Preserve both histories.
7. Return the selected or still-competing design, justified requirements, construction dependencies, and the next authorized realization step.

## Result and consumer

A concrete design and a requirement account tied to the actual selection and use. The artifact may be a name, interface, procedure, representation, or other designed object.

## Quality and defeating case

A pleasing presentation that cannot serve the stated use does not satisfy the design. A requirement inferred solely from an incidental feature remains open.

## Continuation

Continue into construction when the relevant choice and authority are available. Return to exploration when the selected form cannot meet a consequential condition.

## Illustrative distinction

For Subject Systems, the name and repository arrangement are distinct design objects. The chosen name can be retained while the arrangement changes from shared source placements to custom systems within subjects.

The example illustrates the specification. It is not a prospective performance result.

Specified matters of consideration, their names, boundaries, or relationships.

## Subject scope specification

What determination belongs to the subject with consequential context explicit. Excludes: Naming a topic without stating what is to be considered.

## Subject scope identity

Whether two descriptions specify the same matter under a fixed interpretation. Excludes: Treating different names as proof of distinct subjects.

[Content placement](../systems/content-placement.md)

## Subject scope inclusion

The grounds establishing that every matter admitted by a proposed child definition is admitted by its parent. Excludes: Inferring inclusion from useful contribution or word order.

[Content placement](../systems/content-placement.md)

## Subject scope overlap

Construction of a single specified matter admitted by both proposed alternative definitions. Excludes: A document that merely contains two different matters.

## Subject scope exclusion

The grounds establishing why no specified matter can satisfy both fixed alternative definitions. Excludes: Treating a failed inclusion test as disjointness.

## Subject scope gap

A specified matter covered by none of the candidate scopes within the declared coverage domain. Excludes: Claiming universal completeness because every current row has a home.

## Subject name construction

Construction of a literal general-first name for a specified subject without joining distinct targets. Excludes: Generating a label before identifying its matter.

## Subject name ambiguity

Materially different readings permitted by the proposed subject name. Excludes: Assuming a fluent phrase has one exact meaning.

## Subject name equivalence

Whether two names are aliases for the same defined subject. Excludes: Counting aliases as newly discovered subjects.

## Subject name revision

Replacement of a subject name while recording whether the underlying scope changed. Excludes: Concealing a new definition as a cosmetic rename.

## Subject hierarchy construction

Construction of containment relations among already defined subjects within a stated coverage domain. Excludes: Imposing a tree whose sibling scopes still overlap.

## Subject hierarchy collision repair

Revision of or merge definitions after a single specified matter fits two proposed siblings. Excludes: Choosing a preferred home while retaining the collision.

## Subject hierarchy coverage

Whether every matter in a specified finite test domain is admitted by the hierarchy. Excludes: Inferring coverage of all possible subjects from the finite test.

## Subject requirement necessity

Whether a stated condition is necessary for a specified subject operation or justified result under explicit scope. Excludes: A useful method-specific condition being universally necessary.

## Subject requirement alternatives

Which alternative condition sets can support the same specified result. Excludes: A claim that every listed condition must hold together.

## Subject translation coverage

Which defined matters in a specified source term are preserved by a proposed translation into named subjects. Excludes: Whether the source term is popular or all its claims are true.

## Subject translation equivalence

Whether two fixed subject descriptions pick out the same specified matter under stated conditions. Excludes: Sharing a general operation or being relevant to the same goal.

## Subject development assessment

What specified results, techniques, evidence, and remaining limitations have been established within a subject's bounded scope. Excludes: The subject's popularity or a universal scalar maturity score.

## Subject importance assessment

How resolving a specified matter affects stated goals, costs, risks, or further work relative to alternatives. Excludes: Intrinsic importance independent of beneficiaries or objectives.

## Subject attention measurement

How much documented research or practical effort a subject receives within a declared corpus, period, and counting rule. Excludes: Neglect in the world inferred from an unfamiliar name or this workbook's row count.

## Subject name scope underdetermination

Which consequential parts of a subject's scope its name and available definition leave unspecified. Excludes: Multiple fully specified meanings, which are recorded as ambiguity instead.

## Subject name origin reconstruction

Reconstruction of how a specified subject label acquired its recorded wording and referent from available historical evidence. Excludes: Inventing a psychological motive for the name's author.

## Subject name persistence cause

What sustains a subject label's continued use despite identified scope defects. Excludes: Assuming familiarity, authority or convenience is the cause without evidence.

## Subject name substitution effect

How replacing a subject label changes the inferences, questions or operations people actually perform with fixed underlying content. Excludes: An asserted improvement based only on liking the new wording.

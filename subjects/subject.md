Specified matters of consideration, their names, boundaries, or relationships.

## Subject scope

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject scope specification | Specify what determination belongs to the subject with consequential context explicit. | Naming a topic without stating what is to be considered. |
| Subject scope identity | Determine whether two descriptions specify the same matter under a fixed interpretation. | Treating different names as proof of distinct subjects. |
| Subject scope inclusion | Establish that every matter admitted by a proposed child definition is admitted by its parent. | Inferring inclusion from useful contribution or word order. |
| Subject scope overlap | Construct a single specified matter admitted by both proposed alternative definitions. | A document that merely contains two different matters. |
| Subject scope exclusion | Establish why no specified matter can satisfy both fixed alternative definitions. | Treating a failed inclusion test as disjointness. |
| Subject scope gap | Identify a specified matter covered by none of the candidate scopes within the declared coverage domain. | Claiming universal completeness because every current row has a home. |

## Subject name

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject name construction | Construct a literal general-first name for a specified subject without joining distinct targets. | Generating a label before identifying its matter. |
| Subject name ambiguity | Identify materially different readings permitted by the proposed subject name. | Assuming a fluent phrase has one exact meaning. |
| Subject name equivalence | Determine whether two names are aliases for the same defined subject. | Counting aliases as newly discovered subjects. |
| Subject name revision | Replace a subject name while recording whether the underlying scope changed. | Concealing a new definition as a cosmetic rename. |
| Subject name scope underdetermination | Determine which consequential parts of a subject's scope its name and available definition leave unspecified. | Multiple fully specified meanings, which are recorded as ambiguity instead. |
| Subject name origin reconstruction | Reconstruct how a specified subject label acquired its recorded wording and referent from available historical evidence. | Inventing a psychological motive for the name's author. |
| Subject name persistence cause | Determine what sustains a subject label's continued use despite identified scope defects. | Assuming familiarity, authority or convenience is the cause without evidence. |
| Subject name substitution effect | Determine how replacing a subject label changes the inferences, questions or operations people actually perform with fixed underlying content. | An asserted improvement based only on liking the new wording. |

## Subject hierarchy

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject hierarchy construction | Construct containment relations among already defined subjects within a stated coverage domain. | Imposing a tree whose sibling scopes still overlap. |
| Subject hierarchy collision repair | Revise or merge definitions after a single specified matter fits two proposed siblings. | Choosing a preferred home while retaining the collision. |
| Subject hierarchy coverage | Determine whether every matter in a specified finite test domain is admitted by the hierarchy. | Inferring coverage of all possible subjects from the finite test. |

## Subject requirement

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject requirement necessity | Determine whether a stated condition is necessary for a specified subject operation or justified result under explicit scope. | A useful method-specific condition being universally necessary. |
| Subject requirement alternatives | Determine which alternative condition sets can support the same specified result. | A claim that every listed condition must hold together. |

## Subject translation

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject translation coverage | Determine which defined matters in a specified source term are preserved by a proposed translation into named subjects. | Whether the source term is popular or all its claims are true. |
| Subject translation equivalence | Determine whether two fixed subject descriptions pick out the same specified matter under stated conditions. | Sharing a general operation or being relevant to the same goal. |

## Subject development

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject development assessment | Determine what specified results, techniques, evidence, and remaining limitations have been established within a subject's bounded scope. | The subject's popularity or a universal scalar maturity score. |

## Subject importance

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject importance assessment | Determine how resolving a specified matter affects stated goals, costs, risks, or further work relative to alternatives. | Intrinsic importance independent of beneficiaries or objectives. |

## Subject attention

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Subject attention measurement | Determine how much documented research or practical effort a subject receives within a declared corpus, period, and counting rule. | Neglect in the world inferred from an unfamiliar name or this workbook's row count. |

Predicates or their composition studied across target kinds; local instances remain qualified by their own subjects.

## Relation predicate

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Relation predicate definition | Specify what must hold between identified arguments for the stated relation predicate to apply. | Naming an association without its conditions. |
| Relation predicate direction | Determine what changes when the argument order of the relation is reversed. | Assuming every relation is symmetric. |
| Relation predicate inverse | Define the relation obtained by reversing a specified predicate's argument roles. | Treating inverse as logical negation. |
| Relation predicate negation | Specify what denial of the stated relation rules out under its domain and quantifiers. | Treating not-a-subtype as disjointness. |
| Relation predicate necessity | Determine whether the relation expresses a necessary condition for its stated consequent. | Inferring necessity from one successful route. |
| Relation predicate sufficiency | Determine whether the relation establishes a sufficient condition under its stated scope. | Treating a helpful contribution as sufficient. |

## Relation composition

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Relation composition validity | Determine which relation follows from a specified chain under explicit relation rules. | Treating every connected path as a valid implication. |
| Relation composition domain | Identify which argument domains make the proposed relation composition well formed. | Composing predicates whose intermediate arguments have different meanings. |

## Relation pattern

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Relation pattern extraction | Identify a stated relational structure shared by specified cases while omitting designated incidental features. | Mapping that structure to a further case or claiming that every abstraction is relational. |
| Relation pattern case mapping | Determine which elements of a target case can occupy the roles of a specified relational pattern. | Creating the pattern or establishing that mapped objects share every property. |

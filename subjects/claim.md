The content, support, or recorded acceptance of an assertion. Target facts retain their substantive subject.

## Claim content specification

What an assertion says under an explicit interpretation. Excludes: Establishing whether its assertion is true.

## Claim content reference

What the assertion's expressions refer to in the stated context. Excludes: Checking whether evidence supports the assertion.

## Claim content quantifier scope

Which cases an assertion ranges over and where its quantifiers apply. Excludes: Replacing an all-case claim with a typical-case claim.

## Claim content qualification

The conditions restricting the assertion's applicability. Excludes: Quietly adding a condition after a counterexample.

## Claim content equivalence

Whether two assertions have the same stated content under a fixed interpretation. Excludes: Treating association or implication in one direction as identity.

## Claim truth conditions

Which situations make the identified proposition true under its interpretation. Excludes: Determining what the available evidence warrants.

## Claim truth condition satisfaction

Whether the specified situation satisfies a formalized proposition's truth conditions, with its domain facts supplied. Excludes: Absorbing collection of every substantive fact into a universal Truth subject.

## Claim support identification

Which reasons or observations bear on the specified claim and through what stated support relation. Excludes: Treating every cited source as support.

## Claim support sufficiency

Whether the supplied grounds meet a stated acceptance threshold for this claim. Excludes: Describing the agent's actual confidence.

## Claim support limits

Which conclusions remain unwarranted by the supplied grounds under the stated support rule. Excludes: Claiming that an unsupported conclusion is false.

## Claim support dependence

Which support paths rely on the same premise or source rather than providing independent grounds. Excludes: Counting repeated restatements as independent evidence.

## Claim support defeater

A condition that undermines the specified support relation. Excludes: A generic warning compatible with that support remaining adequate.

## Claim support preservation

Which grounds still support the claim after a specified premise or source correction. Excludes: Discarding all support because one path failed.

## Claim inference validity

Whether the identified conclusion follows from the supplied premises under stated rules. Excludes: Establishing the truth of the premises.

## Claim inference counterexample

Construction of an admissible case with true premises and a false conclusion for the specified inference. Excludes: A case with a false premise.

[Claim inference counterexample search](../systems/claim-inference-counterexample-search.md)

## Claim acceptance status

Whether an identified record treats the claim as accepted, rejected, conditional, or unresolved. Excludes: Establishing that this treatment is justified.

## Claim acceptance confidence

The degree or form of confidence actually represented for the claim. Excludes: Inventing a probability where none is given.

## Claim acceptance alternatives

Which competing possibilities the record continues to leave open. Excludes: Treating an unmentioned possibility as ruled out.

## Claim acceptance revision

Which recorded claim status changes in response to a stated correction. Excludes: Claiming that the corrected record was subsequently used.

## Claim inference specification

Specification of the premises, conclusion, and alleged consequence relation of the argument to be assessed. Excludes: Assessing an unspecified impression that the argument is convincing.

## Claim inference revision

Revision of the identified premises or inference step to address a demonstrated defect. Excludes: Claiming the unchanged conclusion must be false because its old argument failed.

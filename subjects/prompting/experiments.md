# Prompting experiments

Standing: reconstructed experimental formulations from the conversation program. They are candidates for use and comparison, not validated universal interventions or exact copies of named source skills.

## Anticipate the contribution

Before producing the answer, determine the contribution that would make sense in the present exchange: what it should cause the reader to understand, what uncertainty it should address, and what work it makes available next. Then produce that contribution. Where useful, retain the anticipated and actual contributions to investigate a consequential divergence.

Open distinction: forecasting the words that will appear, choosing an intended contribution, and anticipating a user's interpretation may have different effects. Do not force the anticipated user reply to be flattering or invent a weak assistant reply to manufacture a correction.

## Exclude its own continuation

Identify a likely substantive continuation. Keep the actual purpose available and suspend that continuation. Produce a different contribution that still bears on the purpose.

Failure case: different wording that performs the same move does not establish a substantive departure. Irrelevant novelty also fails the preserved-purpose condition.

## Preserve one thing and replace another

- Preserve the intended outcome and replace one consequential assumption or constraint.
- Preserve the question and replace a representation, method, or assumed answer space.
- Invert a constraint and derive what becomes possible under that altered condition.
- Reconstruct an account without its usual vocabulary, retaining the relations needed to determine whether the account survives the reconstruction.

These constraints are experiments. Their value lies in the work they reveal or enable; they do not establish a general obligation to avoid familiar words or methods.

## Try without committing

Develop a materially different approach without first concluding that the current approach is defective. Preserve its conditions and compare what the departure exposes. An incidental observation can supply the starting point.

## Change your mind precisely

Reconsider the conclusion, frame, standard, purpose, method, or allocation of effort that actually determines the next contribution. Retain justified commitments when reconsideration supports them. Changing something for variety alone does not establish better judgment.

## Comparison worth making

Use a substantive task with a real unresolved contribution. Compare the actual transformations and consequences, not just response style or length. Label subjective judgments, observed behavior, inferred mechanisms, and general hypotheses separately. An already-informed sequential imitation is not an independent control condition.

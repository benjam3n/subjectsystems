Means available for a specified use, including their capacities or feasible configurations.

## Resource use identification

What contribution makes an item a resource for the stated activity. Excludes: Treating the item's identity as proof of usefulness.

## Resource use access condition

The conditions under which the activity can actually use the resource. Excludes: Equating existence with access.

## Resource use capacity

The amount or range of contribution available from the resource under stated conditions. Excludes: Assuming unlimited reuse.

## Resource configuration construction

Construction of an allocation of specified means to uses under explicit constraints. Excludes: Claiming feasibility merely because the allocation can be described.

## Resource configuration feasibility

Whether the proposed allocation satisfies joint capacity and access constraints. Excludes: Ranking feasible allocations by desirability.

## Resource configuration bottleneck

Which binding resource constraint limits the specified configuration. Excludes: Naming the scarcest resource without establishing its effect.

## Resource configuration substitution

Whether a replacement resource preserves the specified configuration's required contribution. Excludes: Comparing substitutes without fixing the intended use.

## Resource configuration opportunity cost

The best specified forgone use imposed by the proposed allocation under fixed comparison conditions. Excludes: Counting every imaginable benefit as a cost.

## Resource configuration comparison

Comparison of specified resource configurations under fixed uses and criteria. Excludes: Changing the goal to favor a configuration.

## Resource configuration modification

Production of a revised allocation with changed use or capacity assignments explicit. Excludes: Assuming that every change increases feasibility.

## Resource configuration reconstruction

Reconstruction of an earlier allocation episode through its resource assignments, uses, alternatives, and constraints. Excludes: Treating the reconstructed allocation account as a verbatim recovered record.

## Resource retention selection

Selection of identified resources for retention for specified possible future uses under stated constraints. Excludes: Acquiring resources or assuming retention is always beneficial.

## Resource retention duration

How long an identified resource should be retained under specified use and loss conditions. Excludes: How much storage it occupies or a universal retention schedule.

## Resource retention cost

The costs attributable to retaining specified resources over a stated interval. Excludes: The costs of reacquisition after disposal or total historical acquisition cost.

## Resource disposal consequence

Which future possibilities are lost or changed by disposing of an identified resource under stated alternatives. Excludes: Whether disposal should occur under every objective.

## Resource disposal reversibility

Whether a disposed resource or its required function can be recovered under stated conditions. Excludes: Whether it is worthwhile to recover it or whether any similar resource exists.

## Resource future use likelihood

Estimation of the chance that a specified resource will be needed for a defined use within a stated horizon. Excludes: How valuable that use would be or whether the resource will remain accessible.

## Resource future use value

The value of a possible later use of an identified resource under a stated objective. Excludes: Probability of that use or the costs of storage.

## Resource retrieval cost

The cost of locating and accessing an identified retained resource in a specified storage arrangement. Excludes: Retention cost before retrieval or whether the resource is useful.

## Resource use avoidable cost

The excess resources consumed relative to an available feasible alternative meeting the same specified result standard. Excludes: Declaring creative exploration wasteful solely because a cheaper familiar answer existed.

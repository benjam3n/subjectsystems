# Questions

Standing: Working scope definitions, derived for the present repository; revisable through counterexamples.

The specification of what is asked and the relation between that specification and a proposed answer.

This parent means the union of the scopes defined below. It does not mean everything associated with its short name. Membership concerns what a specified inquiry must settle, not every object or operation it mentions.

| Scope | Matter to settle |
|---|---|
| [Q1 — Question specification](#q1-question-specification) | Settle which distinction is requested, about which referents, under which assumptions, and what would count as resolving it. |

## Q1 Question specification

Settle which distinction is requested, about which referents, under which assumptions, and what would count as resolving it. Includes interpreting an existing question, determining whether two formulations ask the same thing, and constructing or revising a question specification.

**Includes:** Creating questions is a narrower investigation here: determine a question that introduces the intended distinction, including a previously unformulated one. New wording and newly recovered wording are insufficient evidence of a new question. Question construction can use any suitable source family.

**Excludes:** The merits of asking a question belong to V3, or V2 when the standard itself is unsettled. The order of several inquiries belongs to R3. Settling the matter a question asks about belongs to that matter: a question about a preference is not automatically a study of questions.

**Boundary case:** “Does asking whether the meeting occurs request the same distinction as asking when it starts?” No: occurrence and start time have different resolution conditions. “Which start time is correct?” investigates the meeting, outside this initial atlas.

**Next substantive result:** Develop identity conditions for questions with presuppositions, requested examples, and open answer spaces. The meeting example rules out wording-only identity but does not settle all question identity.

## Q2 Answer fit

Settle whether a proposed response addresses the distinction, scope, and required form of resolution specified by a question. Assess responsiveness with the question specification held fixed.

**Includes:** partial answers, evasions, substitutions of a different question, and identifying which requested distinctions a response leaves unaddressed. Treat this as structural fit; satisfying it does not by itself establish correctness.

**Excludes:** Whether asserted content holds belongs to its substantive subject. Whether grounds warrant accepting it belongs to A2. How a recipient interprets a response belongs to E2. Changing what is asked belongs to Q1.

**Boundary case:** A definite but false meeting time has the requested kind of content. A true statement about the weather does not answer the start-time question. Correctness and fit can therefore vary independently.

**Next substantive result:** Develop cases in which a response corrects a false presupposition and thereby resolves the request without supplying the answer form initially anticipated.

# Aims and commitments

Standing: Working scope definitions, derived for the present repository; revisable through counterexamples.

What outcome is intended and what course or stance has been adopted. The content of an aim and the act of adopting it remain separate matters.

This parent means the union of the scopes defined below. It does not mean everything associated with its short name. Membership concerns what a specified inquiry must settle, not every object or operation it mentions.

| Scope | Matter to settle |
|---|---|
| [P1 — Goal formulation](#p1-goal-formulation) | Settle the intended outcome and the conditions that would constitute its achievement. |
| [P2 — Commitments](#p2-commitments) | Settle whether an agent adopts, rejects, defers, withdraws, or reopens an option as a course or stance to act on. |

## P1 Goal formulation

Settle the intended outcome and the conditions that would constitute its achievement. Includes developing a candidate aim, revising its scope, and distinguishing an achievement condition from a convenient proxy.

**Includes:** A proposed goal can be specified before anyone commits to pursuing it. Forming goals is a local constructive investigation here; it is not placed in a sibling containing every kind of creation.

**Excludes:** Favoring an outcome belongs to V1; justifying a standard of worth belongs to V2; adopting pursuit belongs to P2. Means and sequences belong to R2 and R3. Whether an observed world meets the stated outcome requires the relevant substantive evidence.

**Boundary case:** “Does success mean having a repository, or making recovered distinctions affect subsequent work?” changes the achievement condition. “Which sequence will bring that about?” changes the means, not the goal specification.

**Next substantive result:** Use the existing goal-discovery design to develop a goal whose achievement can be distinguished from production of a persuasive description of it.

## P2 Commitments

Settle whether an agent adopts, rejects, defers, withdraws, or reopens an option as a course or stance to act on. Includes decision authority and the conditions under which an adopted commitment remains in force.

**Includes:** The result is a commitment state with its scope. The options and their merits can remain unchanged when a decision is postponed or withdrawn.

**Excludes:** Describing an option belongs to its content scope, comparative merit to V3, and actual initiation or completion to T2. Believing a factual proposition is considered as an epistemic position in A5; commitment here concerns what the agent undertakes or treats as an operative instruction.

**Boundary case:** A user can prefer a name without adopting it, and can adopt a name before the repository has been renamed. These are favoring, commitment, and execution respectively.

**Next substantive result:** Specify how a changed premise reopens a commitment without incorrectly erasing unaffected permissions or decisions.

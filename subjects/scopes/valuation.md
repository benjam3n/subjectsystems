# Valuation

Standing: Working scope definitions, derived for the present repository; revisable through counterexamples.

Favoring, standards of worth, and assessments under those standards. These are not a universal home for every statement called a judgment.

This parent means the union of the scopes defined below. It does not mean everything associated with its short name. Membership concerns what a specified inquiry must settle, not every object or operation it mentions.

| Scope | Matter to settle |

## V1 Contextual favoring

Settle what a particular agent favors, is drawn toward, avoids, or finds interesting, in stated circumstances, and which observed contrasts support that descriptive account.

**Includes:** preference discovery, exceptions, and curiosity understood as interest in finding out. Inquiry interest is a narrower concern here; it does not own the questions or investigations toward which interest is directed.

**Excludes:** Whether the preference deserves endorsement belongs to V2; how alternatives compare under a declared standard belongs to V3; an adopted undertaking belongs to P2. A desire to find out need not be a commitment to investigate.

**Boundary case:** Rejecting “Understanding Machine” after finding the domain unavailable is evidence about that choice in context. It does not establish a universal preference against the word understanding.

**Next substantive result:** Distinguish a preference revealed by alternatives from one produced by the framing or limited range of those alternatives.

## V2 Standards of worth

Settle which qualities should count, why they count, how conflicts among them should be handled, and where the resulting standard applies. Includes constructing and revising criteria and investigating reasons for valuing something.

**Includes:** Values and criterion discovery meet here when both concern grounds of worth. A person's observed endorsement is evidence about V1; it is not sufficient justification of a standard.

**Excludes:** Applying a fixed standard belongs to V3. Content truth and inferential support are not made evaluative matters simply by calling their assessment a judgment; their own questions remain in the relevant substantive scope or A2.

**Boundary case:** “Should the repository name avoid claims of completeness?” concerns a naming criterion. “Does System Kit imply more than Subject Systems under that criterion?” applies it.

**Next substantive result:** Develop a standard from a contrast case and test whether the reason for it survives a different example.

## V3 Assessment under a standard

Settle an item's status, ordering, or comparative merit under a stated evaluative standard, with the comparison conditions explicit. Includes tradeoffs and limits on comparability.

**Includes:** Improvement is a narrower comparison here: a changed result is better than a stated baseline in specified respects under a stated standard. A change in the standard must be reported separately from a change in the item.

**Excludes:** Justifying or altering the standard belongs to V2. Producing the change belongs to its target scope. The explanatory relation of a causal account belongs to A3; adequacy of the changed result to a problem belongs to R2. Learning is not a synonym for getting better.

**Boundary case:** A learned habit can reduce performance; a repair can improve an artifact without any learning. Improvement concerns the comparison, not the history that produced it.

**Next substantive result:** Compare an actual baseline and revision while preserving costs, regressions, and any changed criterion. A favorable verdict must identify what improved.

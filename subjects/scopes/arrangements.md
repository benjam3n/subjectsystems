# Problems and courses

Predecessor scope definition: current literal names and any splits are in the [name-change map](NAME-CHANGES.md). The [named inventory](NAMES.md) supersedes these short labels; the earlier definitions and cases are retained below.

Standing: Working scope definitions, derived for the present repository; revisable through counterexamples.

Specified obstacles, problem–remedy relations, prospective courses, and feasibility constraints. The contents of a goal or the merits of an option are supplied by their own scopes.

This parent means the union of the scopes defined below. It does not mean everything associated with its short name. Membership concerns what a specified inquiry must settle, not every object or operation it mentions.

## R1 Problem formulation

Settle the particular obstruction, mismatch, or unresolved condition relative to a stated requirement and situation. Includes finding that an apparent problem was framed incorrectly.

**Includes:** Problem finding becomes formulation of the difficulty, with the requirement and situation explicit. It need not commit to one remedy or imply that the stated requirement should be retained.

**Excludes:** Determining the aim itself belongs to P1. Favoring it or justifying its worth belongs to V1 or V2. Whether a specified remedy effect addresses the obstructing condition belongs to R2. A bare request for information belongs to Q1 when only its specification is at issue.

**Boundary case:** “The files cannot be found” and “the files are found but their correction is not used” identify different obstructions to usable accumulated work. They demand different remedies.

**Next substantive result:** Develop a formulation whose consequences distinguish retrieval failure from failure to use retrieved material.

## R2 Remedy adequacy

Settle whether a specified change, if obtained, would remove or reduce a stated obstruction or satisfy the affected achievement conditions. The change and its effect account are identified inputs; this scope examines their adequacy to the formulated problem.

**Includes:** incomplete remedies, displaced problems, and outcomes that leave a necessary condition unsatisfied. Distinguish adequacy of the proposed effect from whether an intervention actually produces that effect. This is the problem–remedy relation within practical problem solving. Target-specific adequacy facts, such as an answer’s fit to its question, are inputs from their own scopes. R2 examines their consequences for the separately formulated problem rather than redetermining that fit under the label problem solving.

**Excludes:** Arranging steps belongs to R3, explanatory structure to A3, grounds for an effect claim to A2, and comparative desirability to V3. Interpretation of instructions belongs to E2; experience-dependent changes to B2. Whether a particular change occurs is studied in its target scope. A mathematical proof obligation can belong to A2.

**Boundary case:** Assume a change makes every source file retrievable. That effect addresses missing access but does not by itself resolve failure to use an available correction. The assumed effect is insufficient for the latter problem.

**Next substantive result:** Show exactly which condition a proposed remedy would satisfy and which necessary condition remains. Test the effect claim separately rather than assuming the remedy works.

## R3 Courses and contingencies

Settle how proposed actions or inquiries are ordered, depend on one another, and branch in response to conditions. Includes plans, policies, strategies, and answer-dependent question routes in their shared sense of a prospective arrangement.

**Includes:** Planning and Strategy are merged here where their old definitions overlap. Contingency, horizon, granularity, and competitive context are attributes to examine; they do not automatically create exclusive plan-versus-strategy branches.

**Excludes:** The content of each question belongs to Q1 when that content is the matter being developed. Whether its specified effect addresses the problem belongs to R2. Adoption belongs to P2 and actual progress to T2. A route diagram's legibility is an E1 matter.

**Boundary case:** Two different answers that require different next investigations must remain different branches. Calling the arrangement a strategy instead of a plan does not change its membership.

**Next substantive result:** Use a real preference question route and identify a pair of answers that warrant different continuations, including an unlisted branch if necessary.

## R4 Resource feasibility

Settle which proposed configurations are available or possible under stated capacities, access conditions, costs, and constraints. The resource role is relative to a specified use.

**Includes:** shortages, substitutability, joint feasibility, and allocations as configurations of means. It does not infer that every feasible allocation is desirable or that availability guarantees effective use.

**Excludes:** Recovering earlier content belongs to M1; the resulting availability can be an input here. Live focus belongs to T1. Ranking feasible options belongs to V3. Whether its proposed effect addresses the problem belongs to R2; actual effects require their target-specific investigation.

**Boundary case:** A source file can be recoverable while the proposed operation is infeasible within the available context or execution access. Accessibility of that item and feasibility of the configuration are distinct determinations.

**Next substantive result:** Develop a configuration comparison in which a resource substitution changes feasibility without silently changing the goal.

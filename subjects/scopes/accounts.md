# Claims and accounts

Standing: Working scope definitions, derived for the present repository; revisable through counterexamples.

The meaning, support, explanatory or predictive relations, and represented epistemic position of assertions. This family does not acquire every subject merely because it can be described in a sentence.

This parent means the union of the scopes defined below. It does not mean everything associated with its short name. Membership concerns what a specified inquiry must settle, not every object or operation it mentions.

| Scope | Matter to settle |
|---|---|
| [A1 — Claim content and truth conditions](#a1-claim-content-and-truth-conditions) | Settle what an assertion says and which conditions would make it true or false under the stated interpretation. |
| [A2 — Grounds and consequence](#a2-grounds-and-consequence) | Settle what a body of reasons, observations, premises, or assumptions warrants, and the limits of that support. |

| [A4 — Predictive relations](#a4-predictive-relations) | Settle what an account forecasts for a specified outcome before that outcome is available to the predictor, and how the forecast relates to the observed outcome when it becomes available.. |
| [A5 — Epistemic position](#a5-epistemic-position) | Settle what an identified agent or record treats as accepted, rejected, conditional, or unresolved about a specified matter, including which alternatives remain represented.. |

## A1 Claim content and truth conditions

Settle what an assertion says and which conditions would make it true or false under the stated interpretation. Includes reference, scope, conditionals, and the general concept of truth.

**Includes:** The question is semantic: identifying the assertion and its truth conditions. This is distinct from establishing whether those conditions actually obtain. A claim about goals still concerns goals when the inquiry asks what the intended outcome is.

**Excludes:** Whether grounds warrant a conclusion belongs to A2. Question identity belongs to Q1. General encoding or arrangement of an already identified content belongs to E1. Truth of an empirical claim is investigated in the subject the claim concerns, not automatically here.

**Boundary case:** “All local specifications have been executed” differs from “all local specifications are written.” Determining that difference is A1; inspecting the execution evidence is a further substantive inquiry.

**Next substantive result:** Preserve quantifiers and conditions when a source claim is summarized or used in a descendant system.

## A2 Grounds and consequence

Settle what a body of reasons, observations, premises, or assumptions warrants, and the limits of that support. The proposition or account being supported is identified separately.

**Includes:** Inferential support is a narrower investigation here: whether the specified conclusion follows from the specified premises and interpretation. Defeating an inference does not by itself prove its conclusion false. Mixed observational and inferential support remains one support inquiry; it is not forced into exclusive source-type bins.

**Excludes:** What is asserted belongs to A1. Whether an account explains or forecasts the stated target is the relation considered in A3 or A4. An agent's recorded acceptance or unsettled alternatives belong to A5; acceptance is not warrant.

**Boundary case:** TruthFinder-derived inference challenge asks for a case with true premises and a false conclusion. A caution compatible with the conclusion does not defeat that inference.

**Next substantive result:** Apply the existing inference-challenge design to a consequential inference and retain the attempted defeating case, including a failure to resolve it.

## A3 Explanatory relations

Settle how an account makes a specified occurrence, pattern, or dependence intelligible: what the account says it depends on, and which explanatory contrast it addresses. State the account's assumptions while examining this relation.

**Includes:** distinguishing a mechanism, a restatement, a correlation, and an explanation of a different contrast. An account can have an explanatory structure without sufficient evidence for accepting the account.

**Excludes:** Support for believing the mechanism belongs to A2. A forecast for an as-yet-unobserved target belongs to A4. Whether a particular agent can use the account belongs to B1. Writing the account belongs to E1 only insofar as the matter concerns its expression.

**Boundary case:** An account of why a method was unused can differ from a forecast of whether it will be used next time. One model can supply both relations; the two determinations remain distinct.

**Next substantive result:** Construct competing accounts of one failure to use an available method and identify the difference an observation could discriminate.

## A4 Predictive relations

Settle what an account forecasts for a specified outcome before that outcome is available to the predictor, and how the forecast relates to the observed outcome when it becomes available.

**Includes:** forecast targets, horizons, distributions or alternatives, and forecast error under a specified outcome definition. Prediction is not restricted to causal explanation, and explaining an already known outcome does not establish prior predictive success.

**Excludes:** Support for accepting the predictive account belongs to A2. Its explanatory relation belongs to A3. Selecting an action in response belongs to P2 or R3. Evaluating forecasts under a newly disputed standard requires V2 as a separate contribution.

**Boundary case:** A preference hypothesis can predict a later selection without explaining why that preference exists. Record the prediction before treating the later choice as predictive evidence.

**Next substantive result:** Compare an explicit prediction with an actual later result while preserving what was known when the prediction was made.

## A5 Epistemic position

Settle what an identified agent or record treats as accepted, rejected, conditional, or unresolved about a specified matter, including which alternatives remain represented.

**Includes:** descriptions of uncertainty, purported knowledge, and changes in recorded acceptance. This descriptive scope does not certify that the agent's confidence or claim to know is justified.

**Excludes:** Warrant for acceptance or for a claim to know belongs to A2. Mere storage belongs to M1. Usable grasp belongs to B1. What an addressee took a contribution to mean belongs to E2; A5 concerns its accepted or unresolved status with that interpretation fixed. An unanswered question has a Q1 specification even if nobody has recorded uncertainty about it.

**Boundary case:** A document can confidently assert an unsupported result. Its recorded acceptance and its inadequate grounds are separately determinable facts.

**Next substantive result:** Represent unresolved alternatives without silently replacing them with either a categorical verdict or an invented probability.

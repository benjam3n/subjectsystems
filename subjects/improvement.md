Comparative betterment of a changed item against a baseline under a specified standard. The change process is a separate subject.

## Improvement comparison

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Improvement comparison baseline | Specify the earlier item or state against which improvement is claimed. | Selecting the baseline to hide an earlier better state. |
| Improvement comparison criterion | Specify the fixed standard under which the changed item is judged better. | Inferring improvement from change alone. |
| Improvement comparison result | Determine which stated respects are better, unchanged, or worse against the baseline. | Reporting only the favorable differences. |
| Improvement comparison criterion drift | Determine whether an apparent improvement depends on changing the evaluative standard. | Treating a changed criterion as an improved artifact. |

## Improvement effect

| Subject | Definition | Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Improvement effect persistence | Determine how long the demonstrated betterment survives under specified conditions. | Assuming an immediate gain persists indefinitely. |
| Improvement effect transfer | Determine whether the betterment also holds in a specified changed context under a comparable standard. | Generalizing from one favorable example. |
| Improvement effect attribution | Determine what explains the identified comparative betterment. | Assuming the latest intervention caused it. |

# 0004 — Functional groups above subjects

Standing: Rejected as the subject classification by the user's subsequent correction. Superseded by [0005 — Non-overlapping subjects](0005-non-overlapping-subjects.md). The account below records the earlier implementation; its instructions no longer govern new placement.

## Change and criterion

The user requested a level between Subjects and the individual subjects, grouping those that do the same sort of thing. The main navigation now has the form **group → subject → particular inquiry or purpose → custom system**.

Groups are formed around a shared contribution: choosing purposes and commitments, finding out, developing accounts, keeping material usable, creating alternatives and solutions, carrying out work, shaping interactions, and developing capability or quality. Similar names alone do not establish a shared function. Group pages state what each member contributes and where the grouping stops being sufficient.

The existing roster mixes activities, conditions, bodies of knowledge, and whole disciplines. Functional grouping supplies a useful way into this material without claiming that all members have the same logical type. Truth does not become a method merely because it is placed near Reasoning. Philosophy does not become one procedure merely because it is placed near Questions.

## Arrangement and exceptions

The [subject index](../subjects/README.md) now opens eight functional group pages. The folders implement the same layer: `subjects/<group>/<subject>/`. Custom designs live at `subjects/<group>/<subject>/systems/` and keep their subject-specific purposes and source lineage.

Each subject has one main home, with explicit links for contributions across groups. This avoids maintaining several divergent copies of the same subject. A home is an organizing choice, not exclusive relevance, a logical dependency, or proof that the subject is exhausted by the group's function.

Promoting remains in `subjects/unplaced/promoting/` until its intended meaning can support a placement. Awaiting placement is not a ninth functional group. Connections and Improvement remain proposed subjects; nesting them changes their location, not their standing. Education and Collaboration remain excluded as standalone subjects. The earlier reconsideration of Integration, Evaluation, Automation, and Self-improvement remains open.

## What changes and what is preserved

The subject pages and their ten custom design specifications move together. Local navigation, source-profile links, current architecture, and maintenance instructions follow the new paths. Each previous starting investigation remains available within its group's subject table and its subject page. The original source snapshots and historical reviews keep their recorded bytes.

The source-family index remains separate. A group does not inherit one general SDS, QuestionRoute, or other procedure that its subjects must all use. The customization decision remains in force: local purposes can require different objects, operations, results, and standards even within the same group.

## How this arrangement can be revised

Move a subject when another home better explains its shared work; add a connection when its existing home is useful but hides a contribution elsewhere. Split a group when its members need substantially different entry questions that the group page obscures. Merge groups when their distinction no longer helps find or develop the work. Preserve a genuinely unresolved placement rather than inventing a scope to fill the tree.

Useful cases include finding the existing custom system for a task, noticing a previously hidden contribution, and continuing after a finding changes a goal or criterion. Shorter navigation by itself does not establish the best intellectual taxonomy. No global optimum or fixed number of groups is asserted.

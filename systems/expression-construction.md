Direct expression of a subject or operation with the distinctions required for its use.

1. Establish what the reader needs to identify, distinguish, infer, or perform from this entry. Use the accepted task and the content's actual dependencies.
2. Draft the substantive description or operation directly. Use the subject or system name when a heading is needed to distinguish entries.
3. Locate every condition that changes the claim or action. Place it with the proposition or operation it qualifies.
4. Remove a sentence or label provisionally. Compare the resulting interpretations and available actions with the original. Retain it when its removal loses a required distinction, creates a consequential ambiguity, or prevents the intended use.
5. Compare the remaining form with the source and intended content. Repair semantic drift caused by compression, including altered scope, modality, authority, or unresolved status.
6. Read the result without its construction notes. Inspect whether its sequence, naming, grouping, and level of detail express the intended priorities.
7. Challenge the apparent simplicity. Check whether the reader now has to invent a missing condition, retrieve a basic distinction from several files, or interpret a compressed slogan.

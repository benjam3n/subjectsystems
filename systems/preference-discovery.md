Subject: [Preferences](../subjects/preference.md).
Source: [SDS](../sources/systems/sds.md).

## Purpose

Distinguish what someone favors in a stated context without inferring a general preference from liking one whole example.

## Modification

Generate diagnostic contrasts between preference hypotheses. Select the next examples for what their possible judgments distinguish, rather than solely refining an increasingly attractive artifact. Convergence produces a contextual account of preference, not a final design.

## Object

Presented examples; actual judgments; context; competing preference hypotheses; features varied together; unanswered comparisons; exceptions.

## Operation

1. Record the actual candidate, judgment, and context. Keep an explicit statement of preference separate from an explanation inferred from a selection.
2. Construct competing accounts of the judgment that predict a different selection somewhere. If the accounts do not yet differ on any comparison, develop the missing distinction.
3. Construct candidates that separate those predictions while holding other consequential features stable where possible. If they cannot be held stable, record the ambiguity the comparison leaves.
4. Use the actual answer. A tie, rejection of both, context-dependent answer, or inability to choose remains a possible observation; no answer is invented for the person.
5. Revise the accounts that disagree with the observation. A changed context may require a conditional account rather than a declaration that the person is inconsistent.
6. Test whether a favored feature remains favored when placed in another candidate, or whether its value depended on the whole example.
7. Return explicit preferences, supported conditional hypotheses, competing explanations, and the next comparison that would distinguish what remains.

## Result

An evidence-linked contextual preference account and any unresolved discriminator. It can inform another system without being promoted to a permanent user trait.

## Failure condition

Every attributed judgment must have an actual source. A hypothesis remains open where another account explains the same choices. Programmed confidence or repeated approval does not establish calibration.

## Continuation

Stop the current elicitation when the available distinction is sufficient for its actual use, or retain the missing judgment for later. Continue when a consequential choice requires information the current account lacks.

## Case

System Kit was liked, then a lower claim was requested. This supports the specific revision about naming. It does not prove that every word in the chosen name was preferred independently.

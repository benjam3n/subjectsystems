Discrimination between accounts of a contextual preference through contrasts among selected features.

1. Record the actual candidate, judgment, and context. Keep an explicit statement of preference separate from an explanation inferred from a selection.
2. Construct competing accounts of the judgment that predict a different selection somewhere. If the accounts do not yet differ on any comparison, develop the missing distinction.
3. Construct candidates that separate those predictions while holding other consequential features stable where possible. If they cannot be held stable, record the ambiguity the comparison leaves.
4. Use the actual answer. A tie, rejection of both, context-dependent answer, or inability to choose remains a possible observation; no answer is invented for the person.
5. Revise the accounts that disagree with the observation. A changed context may require a conditional account rather than a declaration that the person is inconsistent.
6. Test whether a favored feature remains favored when placed in another candidate, or whether its value depended on the whole example.
7. Return explicit preferences, supported conditional hypotheses, competing explanations, and the next comparison that would distinguish what remains.

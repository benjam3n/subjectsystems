Development and revision of a judgment standard through consequential differences between cases.

1. State what kind of judgment is being developed: for example preference, validity of inference, fitness for use, or contribution to a purpose. Preserve unresolved disagreement over that target.
2. Collect the cases that distinguish the live proposed standards, including a case where an initially plausible standard gives a problematic verdict.
3. Generate alternative standards that account for the contrast. Derive what each would require in a different case before accepting it.
4. Separate an observed verdict from its justification. A user judgment can establish what the user chose; a claim of validity requires its own grounds.
5. Apply the candidate standards to the contrasting cases. Keep a contextual rule or an unresolved conflict where a single rule would erase a consequential difference.
6. Revise the standard or its scope and reopen the verdicts that depended on it. Leave unaffected factual claims and independent grounds intact.
7. Return the criterion, the comparison that motivates it, its grounds and scope, and the verdicts requiring reconsideration.

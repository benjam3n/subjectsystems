Selection of a beneficial change in the present situation and of the means capable of producing it, with both choices available for revision.

1. Recover the actual request, prior corrections, and available evidence of the present situation. Keep the person’s statements separate from inferred needs. A missing observation remains unknown.
2. Identify the change assumed by the response being prepared and the means already being favored. Reopen both choices. Belief revision, explanation, an artifact, and action each require a reason in this situation.
3. Consider the consequential alternatives: what becomes noticed, available, understood, remembered, expected, trusted, valued, felt, possible, or performed. Discover further kinds when these do not fit. Examine the model’s current interpretation and conduct as well as the recipient’s situation; do not infer shared mechanisms.
4. Use the conditional contributions and their supporting subjects to locate a usable contribution. Read the actual content and perform the needed operation. A keyword match or a named subject is insufficient. Construct a contribution when the stored material does not fit.
5. Instantiate the candidate contribution before describing its virtues. Compare its expected benefit, delay, reader work, side effects, and need for further correction with a materially different relevant contribution. Scale this comparison to the task; do not force a simple answer through a displayed checklist.
6. Choose using the person’s stated purpose and the defensible consequences of the work. Preserve uncertainty about benefit. If a missing fact would change the choice, obtain that fact when warranted; otherwise proceed with a justified, reversible contribution. An exploratory encounter can improve the target before a specific target is known.
7. Pass the selected change, the actual means, the applicability conditions, and the scope of uncertainty into Mind change realization. Do not replace these with an intention to be helpful or a universal numerical score.

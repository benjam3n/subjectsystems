Selection, arrangement, execution, and revision of changes to a model, a person, their interaction, or the conditions in which they think and act.

Mind change includes changes of time, duration, pace, order, location, environment, bodily readiness, attention, perception, representation, knowledge, uncertainty, trust, emotion, motivation, goals, priorities, identity, memory, skill, available possibilities, decision rules, action, relationships, tools, resources, and the way later changes are selected. These overlap and do not exhaust the possibilities. Distinguish a person's state, a model's working state, and their shared conditions wherever different operations or evidence follow. For a model, changes to context, retrieved material, representation, instructions, tools, and working assumptions are available only through the host's actual capabilities; a conversation does not establish a change to model weights.

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Beneficial direction | Ground improvement in the person's expressed or contextually supported concerns, the relevant facts, and the consequences. Understanding, experience, exploration, and capability can matter without producing immediate action. Keep inferred purposes revisable. Agreement, intensity, novelty, confidence, activity, and satisfaction do not alone establish benefit. |
| Choice of what changes | Choose the recipient, locus, direction, extent, duration, and conditions of the change as well as the technique. Preserve a useful state when changing it would add cost or damage. More dimensions changed is not automatically better. An unfamiliar starting point can be explored before the problem or destination is settled. |
| Open selection | The current interpretation, problem, criteria, method inventory, medium, and selector remain open to revision. A method earns its place through the operation it can perform here. Neither catalog membership nor a keyword match settles relevance. Missing evidence of benefit can justify a bounded informative trial; it is not evidence of no benefit. |
| Whole arrangement | Judge operations together when one enables, obstructs, or changes another. Preserve required prerequisites and result-dependent branches. Include effects on later opportunities and on the ability to recognize, choose, perform, and revise future changes. Do not claim broad transfer without identifying the uses that could change. |
| Total cost | Count model work, user attention, reading, interruption, choice, execution effort, delay, opportunity cost, switching, recovery, and maintenance when consequential. Compare with the strongest available ordinary response and, where informative, waiting or leaving matters unchanged. A fast trivial gain need not beat a slower consequential gain. Do not invent a universal score or numerical weights. |
| Agency and factual integrity | Keep the person able to inspect, disagree, decline, and redirect. Do not manufacture agreement through deception, concealed pressure, or dependency, or change their priorities merely to make the intervention look successful. Reconsidering a goal or value is allowed; its benefit needs a basis beyond increased compliance. The same standard applies when the model's own selection criteria change. |
| Observed effect | Separate a proposed mechanism, a predicted effect, a performed attempt, and an observed consequence. Use evidence suited to the change: self-report can establish reported experience; a task can reveal a distinction or capability; actual conduct can show uptake. None automatically establishes the others. Model agreement with itself is not independent confirmation. |
| Time and context | Allow benefit to appear, decay, reverse, or transfer over time and across settings. Compare against what would change anyway. Immediate ease does not settle later usefulness, and durability is required only when the intended use requires it. Prefer evidence obtainable through the ongoing work before adding assessment burdens. |
| Correction and continuity | A correction must alter an applicable later choice or operation. Preserve the conditions, scope, evidence, and trigger for reuse, including a case where the correction should not apply. Store durable records through available mechanisms when authorized and useful; do not assume future retrieval merely because a record exists. |
| Selection overhead | Apply these obligations in proportion to the live choice. Reuse settled context and criteria. A clear low-cost answer can be delivered directly. Extra diagnosis, comparison, measurement, or construction of another selector needs a plausible contribution larger than its burden. Unknown effects remain unknown; procedural compliance does not certify a universal maximum. |

1. Recover the current request, relevant prior corrections, available material, and actual capabilities. Identify what is observed, inferred, or unavailable about the person, model, and setting. Retrieve resolvable context yourself. Ask for a fact only when its answer would materially change the next contribution and cannot reasonably be recovered; do not make the person choose your method or complete an intake form.
2. Anticipate the strongest response or continuation you would presently choose. Identify the interpretation and selection rule that make it appear appropriate. At a consequential unresolved choice, construct a credible alternative that changes what is being changed, not just its wording. If the governing interpretation excludes a promising direction, temporarily set its continuation aside and examine that direction without first pretending the original is false. Skip manufactured alternatives when no consequential choice remains.
3. Locate a useful next change using the scope and standards. Inspect whether the obstacle is in the model's interpretation or conduct, the person's current state or access, or their conditions of interaction. Keep competing locations when evidence does not distinguish them. If the needed change is unclear, choose an observation, encounter, or reversible exploratory operation that could reveal it; diagnosis and a fixed destination are not prerequisites for exploration.
4. Consult the conditional contributions and their supporting subjects when they fit the live situation. Retrieve an existing operation when its actual input, transformation, and result fit the situation. Read the exact source before claiming to execute a named original procedure. Adapt or construct an operation when the available ones leave a consequential gap, keeping the adaptation distinct. Consider a different representation, timing, setting, tool, role, or allocation of work when these could change what becomes possible. A capability or authorization gap changes what can be performed now.
5. Construct the viable arrangement. For each consequential operation, identify what it consumes, what changes, what later operation uses that change, and what result would redirect the sequence. Compare meaningful orderings when they can differ. Include preparation, a pause, repetition, simplification, removal, or stopping when warranted. A prerequisite may earn its cost through a later operation even when it has little immediate visible effect.
6. Select using the supported benefits, competing consequences, and total costs over the relevant horizon. Eliminate a route when another is at least as good on every supported relevant comparison and better on one, with no unresolved material trade-off. Otherwise preserve the trade-off and choose on the best supported current priorities. Where uncertainty would reverse the choice, obtain a discriminating observation if its likely value warrants the cost; otherwise make a bounded reversible choice and retain the uncertainty. Do not turn estimates into measurements.
7. Perform the selected change through available, authorized means. Revise the model's working interpretation, representation, search, context, or operation before addressing a problem caused there. Construct the representation, inspect the source, provide the useful distinction, or carry out the task when that is the contribution. Describe a human action when the person must perform it. A proposed relocation or later encounter is not a completed change; a later action requires an actual scheduling or continuation mechanism. Use Mind change realization for the particular demands of producing and delivering the selected contribution.
8. Compare the consequence with the expected change and the relevant alternative using the least burdensome adequate evidence. Check consequential effects on other dimensions, including future revisability. Keep natural change, changed criteria, new resources, and other competing explanations visible when attributing an effect. If a criterion itself changes, preserve the earlier comparison and justify the revised criterion separately. A worked model example establishes only what happened in that example.
9. Use the result to continue, alter the operation or order, change the locus, revisit the concern or criterion, obtain missing evidence, or stop. A rejection reopens the model of the situation; it does not automatically call for stronger rhetoric or repeating the same method. Stop when the requested work is sufficiently handled or the next supported marginal benefit no longer warrants the burden; defer when the opportunity is genuinely better later and a feasible continuation exists.
10. Retain only the reusable situation, operation, observed consequence, scope, and future trigger needed for subsequent use. Propagate corrections to the rule and dependent work that produced the failure. Check the next suitable recurrence and a boundary case when available. Present the contribution in the form the person can use, with material reasons or uncertainty; keep method-selection bookkeeping out of the response unless it helps their decision or they request it.
11. Use the present model to perform these operations when it has the required capacity. If a different available system would materially improve the work, give it these standards, the concrete situation and source material, relevant corrections, known constraints, available operations, and the next decision it must resolve. Require the operation or contribution and its material basis, allow revision of a mistaken target or arrangement, and integrate the actual result. Handoff cost competes with doing the work directly; creating another selector is not an obligatory recursive step.

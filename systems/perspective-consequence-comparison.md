Construction and comparison of perspectives through the differences they make to the same piece of work.

1. Fix an actual piece of work and recover its governing requirements. Leave the problem formulation revisable.
2. Express the current perspective through what it treats as the object, relevant differences, available operations, acceptable results, and grounds for stopping. Infer these from the artifact and conduct where possible.
3. Change one consequential commitment: the object being organized, the unit of content, the comparison standard, the assumed reader task, the timescale of success, or a restriction on available operations.
4. Construct the resulting alternative on the same piece of work. Preserve the required content so that unrelated improvements do not conceal the effect of the perspective change.
5. Compare the alternatives by the distinctions they preserve, the work they enable, the errors they expose, and the burdens they impose.
6. When two cases are treated alike but require different continuations, introduce the missing distinction. When two named perspectives make no relevant difference in the stated use, treat them as equivalent for that comparison.
7. Examine exclusions. A reason for withholding reliance on a perspective may still permit exploration of it. A failure in one application does not by itself justify exclusion everywhere.
8. Combine perspectives only after assigning each contribution a role. Inspect whether one contribution's assumptions or visible expression obstruct another.
9. Change the problem, criterion, construction operation, or dependency when the comparison reveals a defect there. Preserve the identity of earlier comparisons when their basis changes.
10. Retain a useful transition with the conditions that made it useful. Check whether it affects the next applicable construction.

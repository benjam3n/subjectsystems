Comparison of competing causes and repairs for a rejected result, including defects in the process that reproduces it.

1. Recover a rejected item, the requested correction, and the content that must survive it. Keep the user's correction distinct from an inferred general rule.
2. Construct competing explanations of the failure. Each explanation names a condition whose removal would change the observed result.
3. Apply the proposed repair to a small actual specimen. Inspect which original requirements remain unsatisfied and which new defects appear.
4. Follow any recurring defect backward to the source record, construction decision, generator, or acceptance rule that reproduces it.
5. Select the formulation that accounts for the consequential failures being addressed. Retain additional causes when one repair leaves an independently produced defect.
6. Reopen the formulation when a result contradicts its predicted effect.

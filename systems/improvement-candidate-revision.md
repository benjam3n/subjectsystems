Subject: [Improvement](../subjects/improvement.md).
Source: [SDS](../sources/systems/sds.md).

## Purpose

Develop a better version of a specified object while keeping the comparison, costs, and intended improvement inspectable.

## Modification

Keep a baseline and distinguish selection from demonstrated improvement. Candidate variations are compared under the conditions relevant to the improvement claim, and changes in criteria trigger a new comparison.

## Object

Baseline version; candidate changes; target quality; contextual criteria; observed results; costs and tradeoffs; changed standards.

## Operation

1. Retain the baseline and the specific respect in which improvement is sought. Preserve uncertainty about the standard if discovering it is part of the work.
2. Generate candidates that change a plausible cause of the limitation, including a different approach when local polishing preserves the limitation.
3. Use selections to identify promising changes, recording why they are favored. Do not relabel approval as measured benefit.
4. Compare candidate and baseline using evidence appropriate to the target: a derivation, actual use, observation, or a scoped test. Keep material context differences visible.
5. Determine what became better, what became worse or more costly, and which result remains unresolved. Do not hide a tradeoff in a single unsupported score.
6. If the criterion changes, reconsider the baseline and candidate under the revised standard. If only a proxy improves, reopen the claimed benefit.
7. Retain the chosen change with the actual comparison and the conditions under which another use is warranted.

## Result

A revised object or a retained alternative, plus a scoped improvement claim and its evidence or remaining test.

## Failure condition

A polished replacement, a higher proxy score, and a repeated user approval are not interchangeable with improvement of the target activity.

## Continuation

Adopt the change when its grounds fit the use. Continue investigation when the comparison cannot discriminate the versions or when the improvement introduces an unresolved cost.

## Case

A custom preferences system could produce more attractive candidates yet infer preferences less accurately. That result could benefit Design while failing this improvement target.
